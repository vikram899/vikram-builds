Four Years of Logging Everything

Every kilometer, every meal, every DNF — kept in the same place, the same format, for four years straight. Here's what that habit actually bought me.

I didn't start logging because I'm disciplined. I started because I didn't trust my own memory to tell me the truth about how training was actually going. Memory rounds up. It remembers the good week and forgets the three before it that were mediocre. A log doesn't do that. A log just sits there, correct, whether or not you like what it says.

What the numbers are actually for

The kilometers on the legs, the race count, the DNFs — none of it is a scoreboard. It's closer to a second opinion. On days I feel like I haven't improved at all, the log is the thing that tells me, flatly, that I have. On days I feel unstoppable, it's the thing that reminds me I felt exactly this unstoppable two weeks before a bad injury.

"Kept like a running log, not a highlight reel — updated after every block."

That line ended up on the site itself, because it's the actual rule I hold myself to when I'm deciding what goes in. If it only makes me look good, it's a highlight reel. If it's just what happened, it's a log. I try hard to only keep the second kind.

The unglamorous compounding

Nothing about week one of a four-year log is impressive. It's the two-thousandth entry that starts to mean something, because by then you're not looking at a single data point anymore — you're looking at a shape. Mine happens to trend the right direction, most of the time, which is honestly the best outcome I could have asked for from something this unglamorous.
