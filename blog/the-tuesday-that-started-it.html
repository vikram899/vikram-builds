The Tuesday That Started It

Four years of logs started because I didn't want to quit on an ordinary weekday. Here's the version of that day I don't usually tell.

There was no finish line, no bib number, no reason this Tuesday should have mattered more than the fifty before it. I'd said "I'll start Monday" enough times that the sentence had stopped meaning anything. So I didn't wait for Monday. I just put on shoes that didn't fit right anymore and walked out the door before I could talk myself out of it.

I made it one loop of the block. Barely. I remember standing at my own front door afterward, hands on knees, genuinely unsure if I was going to throw up, and thinking: that's it? That's the thing I've been avoiding for two years?

Why it stuck this time

Not motivation — motivation lasts about as long as the first shower after a run. What stuck was smaller and dumber: I wrote the loop down. Distance, time, how it felt, in a notes app I still use today. Not because I thought it mattered, but because writing it down made quitting the next day feel like erasing something instead of just... not doing a thing.

"The log doesn't care if you feel motivated. It just asks if you showed up."

Four years and a few thousand kilometers later, that's still the entire system. Not a training plan, not a philosophy — a running log that got long enough to become a habit, and a habit that got stubborn enough to become an identity.

I don't think the Tuesday itself was special. I think what's special is that it's the first entry in a log that never stopped, and that turned out to matter a lot more than I expected it to.
