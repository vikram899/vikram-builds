About the race

Bergman is a triathlon series organised by Deccan Sports Club, one of the best Indian triathlon organisers. It runs in different cities, and the races come in a few distances. At Ozar there were two:

  • Olympic distance: 1.5 km swim, 40 km bike, 10 km run. Cut-offs: swim 1:05, bike finish 3:15 (cumulative), overall 5:00.
  • Bergman 102: 2 km swim, 80 km bike, 20 km run. Cut-offs: swim 1:15, bike finish 5:15 (cumulative), overall 8:00.

The series has been held in places like Pune (Ozar), Kolhapur, Mysore and Bangalore.

This edition was in Ozar, near Pune, on 4 October 2026, and I did the Olympic distance, my first triathlon at that distance.

The course in a few lines: where the swim was, how many bike loops, how hilly, what the run looked like.

Why this race and not Ironman Goa?

Since this was my first triathlon, I did not want to start with a full half-Ironman like Ironman Goa. I wanted the Olympic distance, and I wanted a race that was a little cheaper and close to where I live.

Bergman Ozar ticked the boxes that mattered to me:

  • The distance: Olympic, a sensible first step.
  • The cost: a little cheaper than Ironman Goa.
  • Close to home: less travelling and easier logistics for me and the bike.
  • The organisers: Deccan Sports Club has been running triathlons for some time, which matters a lot for a first triathlon.

Weighing all of that together, cost, transport, logistics and the quality of the organisation, this was the right race for me.

Preparation

It all started around March, six or seven months before the race. I had been running for about eight months by then, and that was when I decided to do a triathlon.

I bought my road bike in March. Swimming was a different story. I knew how to swim, but only for fun: a pool with friends, or a villa trip. I had never swum competitively or over any distance. So, also in March, I started learning to swim properly, for long distances.

Fitting all of this around a 9-to-5 job was hectic. I had to plan every week, cancel other plans, and give up most of my social life. The body that stood on the start line was also built over 36 weeks from 26 January 2026; the numbers and photos are on the Route.

A typical week looked like this:

  • Strength: 3 days at home, push, pull and legs.
  • Running: 3 to 4 runs, one long run and 2 to 3 easy ones.
  • Swimming: 2 to 3 sessions, whenever I could get to a pool.
  • Cycling: 2 rides.

1. Training

The goal was not to chase a time. It was to build my engine and finish the triathlon, so I could find out where I stand and only then start proper preparation. So I trained for distance, not speed.

Swimming

I swam two or three times a week, depending on how much time I could find. The monsoon made it harder: a lot of the swimming pools around me were closed for about two months, so I did not get as much swimming in as I wanted.

Coaching, how the long-distance swimming came along, the first time in open water.

Cycling

I rode twice a week, and just for distance: no interval or speed sessions. In the monsoon the roads were risky, so I bought an indoor trainer and did those rides at home.

The Triban, what the rides looked like, the longest ride before race day.

Running

I ran three or four times a week and kept it simple. I did not do any tempo runs or intervals, only distance: one long run and two or three easy ones. The long run was 10 to 12 km, and the easy runs were anywhere from 5 to 8 km.

Strength

I cut strength training down to three days a week, a push, pull and legs split with just three or four exercises each. I trained at home with a pull-up bar, dumbbells and a bench.

When work got in the way

Work also took me to Delhi for three weeks, and that hampered my preparation too. All in all, the preparation came down to planning, and to sacrificing the things I would normally have done.
